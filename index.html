    Week at a glance

    Why this layout: Tuesday is a pure study day with no training. Legs-heavy gym is Monday, the quality run is Thursday, and Friday is upper body only so Saturday's long run starts fresh. Sunday adds an easy recovery run before a light full-body session. Gym blocks are 1 h 45 including travel.

    Running: 3 days a week

    Thursday · Quality

    Intervals or tempo

    10 min warm-up, then 4 × 1 km at 10K effort with 2 min jog, or a 20-minute steady tempo. 10 min cool-down. Alternate each week.

    Saturday · Long

    Build with the ladder

    Easy pace, same route loop when possible. Take a gel or carbs every 40 minutes once the run goes past 75 minutes.

    Sunday · Recovery

    4–5 km very easy

    Short, slow and relaxed, the morning after the long run. Skip it if your legs feel sore, not just tired.

    Long run ladder (km), 12 weeks

    A generic starting ladder with a lighter week every fourth week. Start week 1 at your current longest comfortable run and shift the numbers up or down. If you are new to running, add a few weeks before week 1.

    Protein plan

    Weight shown is an example. Enter yours. The 1.6–2.0 g/kg range is a common sports-nutrition guide for people who lift and run. If you have kidney disease or another medical condition, check with your doctor or a dietitian first.

    Sample training day

    Gym and long-run days: add a carb source at lunch and dinner, and an optional whey or milk shake within an hour after training. Easy days: same protein, slightly fewer carbs. Swap chicken or salmon for tofu, tempeh, lentils, eggs or tuna as you like.
